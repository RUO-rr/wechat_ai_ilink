# Experience Selection Rules

Aggregate evidence-level composite scores by experience while preserving requirement
coverage and narrative coherence.

## Adoption Decision

Use these standard decision values:

- `lead`: high-priority requirement with composite score >= 16.
- `support`: composite score 8-15 or useful complementary evidence.
- `omit`: composite score < 8, irrelevant, risky, unverifiable, or space-inefficient.

### lead

Use when evidence strongly covers high-priority responsibilities, required skills, or
important outputs. Place early and write 2-4 strong bullets.

### support

Use when evidence is relevant but not a main selling point, or when it complements a
lead item. Compress to one bullet or place under skills, outputs, awards, or
supplementary experience.

### omit

Use when content is unrelated, weakly evidenced, generic, risky, unverifiable,
redundant, space-consuming, or likely to diffuse professional focus. Omit by default
unless the user explicitly requests it.

`不利内容` means low relevance, weak evidence, or reduced professional focus. It must
never mean a protected personal attribute.

## Ranking Principles

1. Cover core/required requirements before bonus items.
2. Prefer recent high-relevance experience over early low-relevance experience.
3. Prefer strong outcomes and clear ownership over large quantities of ordinary work.
4. Prioritize directly relevant research projects, work, internships, publications,
   patents, competitions, and tools.
5. Remove or compress completely unrelated content.
6. Avoid selecting several experiences that prove the same narrow point while leaving
   a core requirement uncovered.
7. Preserve omitted evidence in the master profile.

## Resume Compression Rules

When space is limited, remove or compress in this order:

1. generic self-evaluation;
2. low-relevance coursework;
3. outdated or weak awards;
4. duplicate skills already proven in projects;
5. low-score support experiences;
6. excessive method lists without results.

Preserve:

1. eligibility-critical education or degree;
2. strongest target-role evidence;
3. verified publications, patents, or major outputs;
4. high-score project bullets;
5. mandatory technical skills with evidence.

## Risk Screening

Flag but do not automatically include:

- low GPA, failed courses, unfinished degrees, unexplained gaps;
- unrelated short experiences that dilute positioning;
- weak awards or certificates with low relevance;
- unverified claims;
- exaggerated proficiency;
- sensitive personal information;
- publications listed as accepted/submitted/in preparation without evidence.

Handle risky content by:

- omitting it when unnecessary;
- reframing it truthfully when relevant;
- marking it `需用户确认` when uncertain.

## Sensitive Fields

- Chinese resumes may retain gender, political affiliation, native place, date of
  birth, photo, and address only when supplied or explicitly requested.
- Do not infer age from graduation year unless the user asks. Do not include political
  affiliation unless the user provides it.
- English resumes hide photo, gender, age, marital status, political affiliation, and
  full address by default. Use city/country only if location relevance matters.
- Never make a negative selection decision based on sex, age, ethnicity, marital or
  family status, health, disability, religion, or political affiliation.

## Decision Record

For each experience record:

- total and highest composite score;
- high-priority requirements covered;
- evidence quality and recency;
- adoption decision: `lead`, `support`, or `omit`;
- recommended placement and bullet count;
- reason for selection, compression, or omission.
