# Student and Research Candidate Rules

## Student and Recent Graduate

- Prioritize education, expected graduation, favorable verified GPA/rank, relevant
  coursework, research training, projects, internships, competitions, and scholarships.
- Move the strongest target-relevant project above less relevant prestigious items.
- Show personal contribution and outcome, not only project background.
- Omit weak secondary-school details once higher education evidence is sufficient.

## Research Experience

For research, biotech, chemistry, pharmaceutical, and academic roles, prioritize
research outputs, methods, publications, patents, datasets, protocols, and verified
project outcomes over generic self-evaluation.

For research projects, use this bullet structure:

1. research aim / scientific problem;
2. experimental or computational strategy;
3. candidate's personal contribution;
4. key methods, instruments, datasets, or models;
5. result, output, publication, patent, poster, thesis, or reproducible artifact;
6. relevance to target role.

Avoid:

- overstating independent ownership;
- listing techniques without context;
- claiming publication status beyond evidence;
- converting routine coursework into research experience.

Group skills as experimental, analytical/computational, software/programming, domain,
and language. Do not produce an unprioritized keyword inventory.

## Publications and Patents

Use consistent citations and exact status:

- published/online/accepted;
- in press;
- under review;
- submitted;
- preprint;
- manuscript in preparation.

Record author order, co-first/corresponding status only when verified. For patents,
distinguish application, publication, authorization, and grant.

## Academic CV

Academic CVs may include education, appointments, research interests, publications,
patents, grants, teaching, supervision, talks, conferences, service, awards, and
references. Do not force a complete academic CV into the one-page industry template.
